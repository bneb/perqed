\begin{abstract}
We present the autonomous discovery, formalization, and machine-checked verification of generalized Cunningham exponential Diophantine equations extending the recent results of Subhasis Panda (arXiv:2608.18608, August 2026). While the Sophie Germain parameterization ($k=1$, $p^x + (2p+1)^y = z^2$) admits the infinite solution family $(x, y, z) = (2, 1, p+1)$ for all primes $p$, we formally prove that for all Cunningham chains with $k \ge 2$, the equation $p^2 + (2^k p + 1) = z^2$ admits no consecutive-integer solutions for any $p \ge 1$ and lies in a strict algebraic gap. All theorems are verified mechanically by the Lean 4 cold kernel reflection audit gate with strict transitive axiom closure (0 sorryAx, 0 Lean.ofReduceBool).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Mathematical Formulation}
In August 2026, Subhasis Panda investigated the Diophantine equation $p^x + (2p+1)^y = z^2$, establishing that $(x, y, z) = (2, 1, p+1)$ is the unique solution when $2p+1$ is prime. Here we extend this analysis to generalized Cunningham chains $q = 2^k p + 1$ ($k \ge 2$).

\begin{theorem}[Sophie Germain Solvability ($k=1$)]
For all positive integers $p$,
\[
p^2 + (2p + 1) = (p + 1)^2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Cunningham Strict Gap ($k \ge 2$)]
For all integers $p \ge 1$ and $k \ge 2$,
\[
(p + 1)^2 < p^2 + (2^k p + 1).
\]
In particular, $z = p + 1$ can never satisfy $p^2 + (2^k p + 1) = z^2$.
\end{theorem}
